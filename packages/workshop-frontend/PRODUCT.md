# Legal OS — product truth

Legal OS is an autonomous immigration-practice operating system sold to law firms as a private
deployment (one firm per instance), in the market Legora and Harvey occupy. A lawyer opens a
matter, talks to one agent, drops the client's documents in, and the firm's agents read the record,
build the case knowledge, plan, and draft the petition and forms the way this firm drafts, from
playbooks the firm writes and teaches. The lawyer approves, corrects, and directs.

## Who uses it

- Practitioners (attorneys, associates, paralegals) at small and mid-size immigration firms.
  Expert in the law, not in software. They work matters all day; the screen must answer three
  questions in seconds: is it working and on what, what needs me, show me the work.
- Firm admins: one or two people who invite members, set allowances, watch spend, and curate the
  firm's playbooks.
- Clients (beneficiaries): through an unchromed portal link, uploading documents and answering
  plain-language asks. They never see legal machinery.

## Product rules

- Invite only. No public signup. One admin bootstraps the firm.
- Every claim traces to a document and a verbatim quote. Honest states everywhere: a document
  the firm could not read is never shown as read.
- Agents act outward (client messages, filings, exports) only through the attorney's release.
- The UI speaks law, never machinery. No agent avatars, no sparkles, no gavels.

## Voice

Plain legal English, calm and precise. Errors name the problem, state that nothing was lost, and
say what happens next. No exclamation marks, no marketing adjectives inside the product.

## Surfaces

Sign-in and invitation signup; the matters desk; the matter screen (conversation, documents,
evidence, case map, petition workbench, client, messages, desk); the playbook library and Teach
the firm; the admin's firm screens (team and invites, usage and allowances, gatekeepers); the
client portal.

## Anti-references

Generic SaaS dashboards with metric heroes, legal-tech clip art, AI chat toys, dense admin tables,
and the developer-tool look of the platform this is built on.
